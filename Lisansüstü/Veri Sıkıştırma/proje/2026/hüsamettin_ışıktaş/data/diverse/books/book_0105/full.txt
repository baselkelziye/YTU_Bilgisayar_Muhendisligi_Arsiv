**Information theory** is the mathematical study of the [quantification](/wiki/Quantification_(science) "Quantification (science)"), [storage](/wiki/Data_storage "Data storage"), and [communication](/wiki/Telecommunications "Telecommunications") of a particular type of mathematically defined [information](/wiki/Information "Information"). The field was established and formalized by [Claude Shannon](/wiki/Claude_Shannon "Claude Shannon") in the 1940s,[[1]](#cite_note-1) though early contributions were made in the 1920s through the works of [Harry Nyquist](/wiki/Harry_Nyquist "Harry Nyquist") and [Ralph Hartley](/wiki/Ralph_Hartley "Ralph Hartley").

Information theory was initially formed in the context of telecommunication but soon found a wide range of other applications. It is now at the intersection of [mathematics](/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), [statistics](/wiki/Statistics "Statistics") and [computer science](/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), and has applications in diverse fields ranging from [electrical engineering](/wiki/Electrical_engineering "Electrical engineering") and [physics](/wiki/Physics "Physics") to [neurobiology](/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience").[[2]](#cite_note-:2-2)[[3]](#cite_note-:3-3)[[4]](#cite_note-:0-4)
